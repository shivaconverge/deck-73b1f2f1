Appendix · synthetic-data methods — the full map

Present synthetic-data methods are low on coverage.

grow outward from a sample —

Self-Instruct · Evol-Instruct · Self-play · Distillation — more, harder, self-copying · still sample-bound
Persona Hub — a billion viewpoints · changes who asks, not what exists
Verbalized Sampling — surfaces suppressed answers · recovers its own mass, never exceeds it
DCE — remembers and rejects repeats · knows what it drew, not what it missed
AIDE — expands from seed attributes · every step pulled back toward the seed
FAC Synthesis — coverage in feature space · limited to what the model already represents

the ones that verify — verification is free when you have a world to run; business decisions don't —

RST — harder tasks in rounds · needs a sandbox to accept each one
AgentMercury — writes its own verifier · a fixed grid of industries and countries
Induction Labs — rewards novelty directly · maximizes strangeness, not real-world frequency
Graph synthesis — runs only where an ontology already exists

Every current method grows outward from a sample. None of them starts from the space.

Appendix · staged experiments

Test the cause before you commit.

Business history records what happened together — it rarely proves what caused what. Our specialists are trained to treat their own uncertainty as a signal: find the causal claim the decision leans on, look ahead across possible tests, and run the cheapest one with the highest expected information gain — then re-decide on the result. Big bets become sequences of cheap, causal tests.

a capability the specialists are trained for · scored in both seed experiments

Appendix · before you ask

The questions you're already asking.

"Won't the next frontier model make this pointless?"

Better base models make our specialists stronger — we retrain on our data. The moat is the coverage data, not the weights.

"Is synthetic data any good?"

Seed-bound synthetic data circles what it already knows. Ours expands a mapped space with measured coverage.

"Why won't the labs build this?"

They sell generality. And they're buyers of this layer — $1B+/year — not just rivals.

"How do you measure a CEO model?"

Outcomes take years — the decision process is checkable now: facts, constraints, risks, assumptions. That's how boards judge CEOs.

"What if a lab copies the method?"

Every cycle adds coverage nobody else has. A copy starts at zero on a curve we publish.

"What about hallucinations?"

In a generator, imagination is the point — a scenario is a region to master, not a fact to memorize.

"Where's revenue?"

This seed buys the proof; first paid deployments land with design partners around the next round.

"Why this team?"

Training (Gagan) + inference cost (Nitin) + live companies (Avinash). The thesis is the three of us.
